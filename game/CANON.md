## The basin

The Rell basin is a hooked bowl with a navigable inlet to the south and hard land on its north and west edges. The water rises and falls much faster than an ocean tide. The landscape is at the foot of an oscillating inland lake. A continuing fourteen-year drawdown exposed the modern orchard and the iron footsteps on Sill Walk. Most action takes place during the oscillation's present stable pattern. There is daylight over the basin. One mark is the interval between strokes of the regulating bell, long enough for a walk between neighboring landings. It is a game interval, never elapsed real time.

Older dwellings, now the lower households, were built into a natural ledge under the municipal wayhouse. The living terraces squatted above them and built out over the water when tenancy was cheaper than cutting more land. In this district, tenancy and structural load literally share a ledger. The houses on Loom Lane had a land rent to the original ledge, collectible in widening and maintenance. Later, compulsory purchase erased some of the supporting addresses from the public register and renamed their bearing capacity "vacant reserve." The glass-front terrace and west storehouse interests got cheaper floors. No one came down to check the kitchens.

The ordinary bridge on the north lip, the upper local roads, and the houses on dry land are independent of the disputed line. The load in dispute is the extension of the rows over the basin. Line 11 supports pump staging and line 27 supports the movable ferry shelf. Line 19 is the long terrace shunt bearing on the Sixth Pier. The other two traces do not have the distinct residence pads and their tap signatures are geometrically different. A hand tally that says "19" proves nothing by itself.

## Restware

The lining of local yellow stone can be fired into matched glazed faces, called restware. A pair that's seated flush in its own fixed frames can displace compressive load at one beam onto the foundation at the other, up to its rated force. The receiving floor bears the same load the sending bearing sheds. Signal pressure also passes through the pair. An unseated face is inert. Pairs are formed together and cut into keyed shapes. A random plate, shard, or portable weight cannot substitute for a commissioned basin bearing. Restware cannot pull in tension, amplify a force, transmit words, or act over an unlimited gap. The present systems are dry-hung and ordinarily stable. A transient water head while bringing a new bearing onto load can run backward through an open bleed and split it. Test beds take their proof load in compression. The current old seat can hold indefinitely under ordinary tides or shed onto a seated receiver, but it cannot live through being disconnected before the receiver takes load.

An impression from a live sill carries the asymmetric three-point pattern of its service branch and pressure dimples from dwelling pads. The squeeze at the Settlement Shaft takes an impression mechanically from six distinct occupied pads. The pads stay active while the households are there, or under those floors' installed household weights during a warning drill. The recorder measures the pulse from the branch. At high tide its fixed suspension momentarily rides on the mechanism and drags the foil. These are deliberately different sources of evidence. Evidence based on a paper street name, a copied shape, or a dead branch proves neither.

At the east, the existing earth bed is sound when the dipped shoe is held against the arch by water. At the west, the bed is the old quay rock, with a missing heel shim and a bleed downriver. Neither receiver can take a full transfer without the relief gap discharging. Commissioning the east costs the low orchard rows. Commissioning the west ventilates a silt pocket through the storage level. Both outcomes redistribute very real burdens. People can move ahead of the water. The upper orchard, the Long Span, and the Trestle are safe.

## The runner

The player is doing a cover shift as a local relief runner, old enough to work, worn boots and a belt hook. No name, ancestry, prior belief or allegiance is imposed by the fiction. The delivery slip was sent by a clerk who used the old route card and means its plain words. The runner has never been below the Wayhouse and can read ordinary local signs. The filing deadline for a genuinely occupied house is indefinite. Nobody waits for the runner to save them; routine work carries on. Larger events in this slice of the basin have explicit causes.
