# Registry

## `upper_bridge`

Where the old stair stopped, a high bridge carries straight into the Registry. Its caps are one stone each, unjointed and mottled like wet skin. A pigeon walks the edge without ever putting its wings up.

Sight lines reach dry bedrock at both ends of the arch. The bridge is independent of the little mechanical ticks elsewhere. A tiny inspection plate gives surface routes to Bearing Gate from Returns Corridor.

## `registry_entry`

A queue has marked the room in patches of heel wear. The desks have moved since then. A public stack stands north, intake east, hearings west. The floor has the quiet of a building whose weight is paid for.

**Guide:** "Applications for living service at Test Press. A house impression and a current dry draw of the same measure. Issued instruments go to the works, not to Intake." Sile works these rooms in a regular round. Sile can explain that a street number in the book is no proof of a load currently on the line.

## `intake_counter`

The counter slopes away from whoever stands in front of it. A low passage east leads to spare copying stock. A linen map under glass points north to a room full of earlier versions of itself.

The counter takes paper forms. The runner's dispatch directs an occupied return to its address socket. The receiving stamp here was never cut to take an embossed return band from the works.

## `map_vault`

Long vellum sheets hang from floor to ceiling, kept from curling by sewn wooden ribs. Each sheet registers with the next at a series of holes. Between two of them is a service overlay in thinner cloth.

**Overlay:** Line 11 runs to the pumps. Line 27 makes a large loop around the ferry position. Line 19 passes straight through the Wayhouse Foot and the mounting of the Sixth Pier to the suspended rows. In the ordinary season overlay the boxes at the foot are faintly crossed out. The marks aren't a current house impression.

## `dead_letter`

The labels on the dead mail sort by weight rather than street. Some of the bundles are too heavy to lift because the string has picked up a skin of wax over years of repair.

Old letters to No. 6 are all marked "RETURN IF OCCUPIED". The latest genuine dates run past the public closure. The string has been sealed on the faces that would show whether they were read. Pinned notes point west to Pattern Room.

## `pattern_room`

On a low table, two pieces of yellow ware are held apart in separate iron frames. Putting your hand flat on one makes the other tick against its seat. A red iron shoe lies in a marked tool niche.

The sample is a paired demonstration. One side is labeled load and the other bearing. Motion stops if either frame is opened. A cast outline of the shoe says "WEST — heel missing from holdfast. Take by bench handle." The shoe is a tool, intended to be taken and fitted in the missing receiver heel. Its dimensions do not fit the east gland.

## `hearing_floor`

The public seats face a high board. Whatever used to have a chair behind it now has a fly press. A tray under the press has just enough room at either end to hold a strip.

**Press plate:** "Impression from live household sill. Dry scratch trace, same branch. Pull once; return both originals. Issued instrument seats at works." The die distinguishes the shape and pressure dimples from type, wax transfers and ordinary ticket stock. Sile can run it if present and civil. Anybody can pull the hand lever. The originals remain available. Paper signatures have no effect on the steel doors.

**Service writ:** "19. Living load acknowledged. Seat at bearing entry. Route discharge at discretion of attending hand." The metal shows a different stepped pattern from the one on the press.

## `reverse_stack`

The shelving comes to within a hand's breadth of the ceiling. They keep the orders here in reverse, by the number removed from the books. A cabinet starts with unnumbered drawers and ends with a recessed handle marked 6.

Examining the handle shows black writing cloth pinched under its guide. It comes from a carbon order folded inside a false floor in the drawer. Prying lightly with the tapered shim lifts the false floor. An established tool of equal stiffness and thickness works as well. A hard pull leaves the cloth trapped. **Carbon order:** "Vacancy certified by absence of names on forward issue. Collect remaining rent by charge at lower foot." Six rent shares follow. The east shortcut to Public Stack is latched from this side and can be opened locally.

## `returns_corridor`

Crates stand with their repaired ends outward. Every fourth wall stone bears a horizontal file mark where the passage drops toward the Dry Crossing.

Sile walks this corridor for a count before returning to hearings. The east door to the back of the stack is an ordinary public approach. Households don't use this corridor for final service returns.

## `copy_balcony`

A narrow balcony, screened against the weather, smells of sharpened metal. A soft recorder leaf sits on glass under a brass clip. The neighboring room would be too humid for it.

The leaf can be taken. "For live draw; refinish at frame" is stamped on its tail. Its coating yields differently under different pulse patterns. It can be smoothed for another draw as long as it is intact.

## `dry_crossing`

A joint in the uphill flagstones is open to view. Far down inside it you can see the braided forebay drain running separately from the way up to the Trestle.

The flags are above any tide or receiving spill. A stamp on the curb sends bearing workers up toward the Trestle.

## `public_stack`

Numbers have been repainted in a slightly different red. The latest figure is easier to read on the reflection in the glass than on the shelf edge. A latched door on the west wall is labeled BACK OF ISSUE.

Look closely to see which order they skipped, 6. The door is a shortcut from Reverse Stack. A thin rip at the latch height is large enough to admit a probe, but the cast elbow means a straight reach can't raise it.
