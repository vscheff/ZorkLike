# Water works

## `ferry_head`

The landing steps have no rail on the water side. Painted ticks on a fixed stake look like fingertips slipping into the water. A hollow beneath the back step takes the wake of any boat passing the south bend.

The shallow footways begin on neighboring steps, rather than at this landing. Kel can show the tide marks and distinguish the short tender from the packet boat, now outbound well off basin. No boat now sits here to use.

## `catchgate`

A gate stiff with mussels separates a fast channel from water that barely seems to move. Below the safe apron an iron shelf runs east to the tail drains. The slick lines above it are too close to the lip for comfort.

The guard sign shows three blacks, of which two mean get off the shelf and three mean the grating is submerged end to end. At levels 0 and 1 there is ordinary footing. At level 2 the current throws people west over the low gate into this apron; at 3 there is no breath pocket under the guard.

## `pump_deck`

The machinery runs low enough under the grating that you feel it in the knee first. White cones over two intakes catch the spray and return it in straight streams. People in work aprons cross the deck at intervals on a marked circuit.

The deck is on its separate bearing. The lowest cone gives a clear reference to water level if viewed close. Edra distinguishes this pumping system from the old goods cradle, which is northwest of the pump room by its drive.

## `pump_room`

An isolated workbench has had its feet wound in cork. In a dry tray lies a forked pawl with two unworn cheeks. The sign over the bench shows two sections of clutch with the same unequal forks.

The pawl is a replacement for the empty slot in the Winding Gallery. The pumps draw and return water continuously, and throttling their everyday ports cannot change the basin cycle or dry the lower houses. A mechanic may point out that the gallery's chain will also catch both lugs under a static knot.

## `winding_gallery`

A guard over the cradle clutch has been removed and hung from a peg. One fork is missing from the assembly. Someone has chained the guard up with the useful end down. There is a walk southwest to the well and a shorter stair west to the gauge.

The drive accepts the forked spare pawl or a short reeved chain locked into its two lugs. The chain of the latter must stay attached during operation. These are the only initially available assemblies that catch both lugs simultaneously. A single hook will pull one and release the other. A lift started here moves to the called landing in three marks. Movement generates a clear rising or receding grind in the shaft.

## `blind_cradle`

With light: A cage with a standing board wide enough for two pairs of boots. A rattle of thin iron comes from the selector chain at the rail. The cramped sides of the shaft are polished where full cages have gone through.

The raised U and D can be felt on the call plate. An upper landing is UP here, lower DOWN. The cage itself is always above the accumulated water on every part of the track. No intermediate jumping is a safe substitute for the landings. In movement the latch prevents crossing and the car makes progress on every physical mark, no matter where called.

## `sluice_shelf`

The narrow iron shelf is bolted above a darker strip of wall. Small ridges in the iron point seaward. From above, the drain water is almost clear enough to be invisible.

Examining the lower seam discovers toe wear disappearing under an inlet grill and a small captive lifting eye set beyond finger reach. A hook or loop of chain can get it under tension and pull it. It stays above its seat until mechanically returned. The shelf is fast to leave, but an attempt takes a mark here even if the eye sticks. At level 2 on this side the player is thrown back to Catchgate. The dry return duct begins above the inlet throat.

## `brine_lip`

Water has laid a brittle collar on the bank under the rail. Its earlier collars are visible deeper down, gray at the inside, clear at the edge. The safer path southeast follows the rail above the fast drain.

The smell here tells a worker it is pre-rise or post-fall, but only a close view of the bolts shows which. No loose lumps of bearing material can be made from the salt collar.

## `gauge_nook`

A straight board on a float rides inside a slot in the wall. The old working scale is still marked with tenon cuts. Somebody added the bell numbers at each cut a long time later.

**Tidal scale:** "Mark 0–2 slack below first cut; 3 first black; 4 second; 5–7 third; 8 second; 9 first; 10–11 slack. Count repeats." First black or below is safe for a dry trace or a live transfer. The scale explains when the tide is rising. Cross-reading three full marks is required by both machines. Report phase and level, no future complication.

## `tail_drain`

Fast water passes under a fixed bridge no wider than a crate. The dark culvert mouth below the bridge joins the bottom end of Sill Walk. Rope worn between two timbers shows where people pulled out someone already in the drain.

Standing on the bridge is safe throughout the cycle. The dry walk northwesterly to Brine Lip is independent of the low shelf west and the culvert south. The culvert route to Wash Step can bypass the main paths, with the same danger to gear.

## `pond_hut`

A shed with racks for dipped paper. The only wet rack in winter is the one used to note the timing of small pulses: rain, pump, ferry. A north door has been lined with felt to keep out the working noise.

An old sample shows three entirely different patterns from the three service loops. It is a teaching copy. Just bringing it to the hearing yields no impression from a live circuit. Edra has argued with the office for a station box over the leaf rack.

## `mussel_stair`

The narrow treads are scarred with mussel knives. There is an iron mouth rising next to each tread, bolted from underneath; at high water these give off a bubbling note.

This stair is dry on its upper bench. The lower ends of the mussel ropes are fixtures, not movable strong arms for the cradle. Kel can be caught between here and the landing on an ordinary round.

## `recorder_room`

It is quiet enough here to hear pressure ticking at different distances. The scratch frame has a spring clip for a soft leaf, a dial marked 11, 19, and 27, and a counting wheel with three fingers. A little float in a glass tube repeats the black cuts of the big gauge.

**On the frame:** "A clean leaf. One line. Three marks below second black. See all fingers drop before withdrawing." The slot is too thin for an ordinary ticket or a scrap of carton. The loop can be set locally and a recording erased by starting again. Withdrawing an active leaf cancels that draw; inspect the wheel for remaining marks and feel the after-motion disappear in a complete draw. An honestly blurred leaf shows drag to one side. Line 19 prints a pair of close pricks followed by a wider single gradation. Line 11 has an even saw; line 27 has a long drag between paired dots.
