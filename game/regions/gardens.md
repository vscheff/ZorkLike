# East orchard

## `orchard_gate`

The gate sticks open against a peeled branch. A rail to the east leads back to the ferry office. Stone tags on the orchard side give names to fruit set out in the dry years.

The first tags are ten years older than their trees. They survived the move down from the old orchard wall. Van passes this gate repeatedly with a sack of loose labels.

## `rain_orchard`

Each branch trained against the rain has a short cloth tail. On the lower rows the ground is flat enough that the trunks seem to grow out of a horizontal shadow. Higher up, the separate stone works of the nursery are visible to the south.

This walk is the upper service path. Moving people and hand tools above it is possible under warning. After east discharge the soil below the path is covered and bits of cloth hang half in the water.

## `glass_path`

Thin wedges of old window glass show where grit has been put down for footing. At the bend a stair runs under the wall instead of stopping at it.

The plants here are sized against marked cuts in the retaining stone. The reading is for trunk thickness. The seed loft is up along the path to the north.

## `seed_loft`

Sun makes a shelf almost as hot as a stove just below the tiles. On a cool lower bracket sits a vented wooden box chalked BITTER PEAR. Damp stems hang on the drying wall.

There is one viable batch of graft stock in the box. The loft is high above the old orchard, but the relief culvert runs right under the subfloor and will steam the cuttings if east discharges. Shed Roof is the designated dry place, per Van and a white board on this bracket. Moving the rootstock and warning people are independent.

## `packing_shed`

Small candles of yellow wax hang from the ceiling around wood frames. A coil of waxed packing cord sits where it won't pick up dirt. The whole south wall is a row of outlets onto the Rain Trough.

Van left the cord for the bent water gland. It fills the wheel's gap evenly. A waxed rag makes a rough but stable gland. Plain cloth saturates at first pressure.

## `stone_nursery`

Grafts take here in boxes that look like closed tool trays. The soil is cool enough that the gardeners warm their hands on the brick rims above it instead.

The nursery is safely uphill and can cradle wet cuttings too late to save a year's yield. Van can name the proper place for the prepared bitter pear stock.

## `east_berm`

On the inside of the bank a pattern of wetness is shaped like a rising hand. A lower black channel has been cleared all the way down to the first row of pears. The receiving house is south along the top of the berm.

The bank has a stone mark where relief water will go when the bed takes sustained load, and it leads through the low trees. The berm is safe through either discharge.

## `east_head`

An opposing pair of blocks meets at a narrow mouth in the stone. A brass jack bears against the smaller block. A float on the jack pushes beads up its stem.

Three beads up means full charge. The plate shows a shoe seated under the arch after the third bead rises. Holding the jack with anything but the charged water leaves the smaller block cocked. Once seated its captive lock retains the charge. The relief channel goes east of the house below foot level and splays into the low orchard. The house itself stays dry.

## `root_stair`

With light: Cut roots bracket a stone stair. Below the bottom bend is a grille studded with pegs at the elbow, much newer than the stair. Beyond it the stair continues at the same grade.

The thumb latch is on the inside, around a closed edge. The bars have no working clearance from above. Calling through can only get access if a resident is here and physically lifts it; no initial schedule reaches the gap. A runner below can lift the thumb lugs. The passage is wholly above the wash.

## `rain_trough`

The bottom of a wooden channel shows the grain where it swelled. A quarter-turn valve has leaked enough to grow a dark fringe in every direction. Two guide wires head west to the receiving house at different heights.

The gland has a split visible in the packing. The handle has the shapes for flow and shut. The open valve with a sound pack catches water each mark of second black or higher. The float retains its rise while the valve stays open. Shut before seating, it bleeds a division at slack water. The jack reports how far the charge has reached. At three divisions, the jack can be seated by hand.

## `shed_roof`

The roof juts into a patch of wind and keeps even its north tile dry. Timbers at the east end have steps down to the ferry office. On the far rail somebody made a shelf to keep supplies above the worst water the trough could throw.

Stock here is secure from either discharge. Mention the seed box only if it has actually been placed here. The south steps return to the upper orchard path.
