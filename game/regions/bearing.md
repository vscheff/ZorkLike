# Bearing works

## `upper_trestle`

The trestle is filled with dry air from a crack in the hill. North stands a steel gate, east is a square cut of white light, and west a bridge wide enough for one person to stand sideways on.

Load travels away through deep stone here. No bearing release cuts the outside approaches. A shear line visible below the rail extends as far as the edge of the occupied ledge.

## `bearing_gate`

The front of the gate is undamaged except around a metal slot with six stepped levels. There is a line of guide teeth on the inside for a punched strip to push against.

Seat a genuine writ and the lock stays released. The strip comes back with an added notch. The same latch bar frees the doors between Daywell and Selector Aisle and between Grate Road and Keel Walk. It resists a single straight probe. The outside roads remain unaffected by the relief.

## `balance_deck`

Rows of long flat beams end a thumb's breadth short of each other. Feet sound much too light here. White paint on the floor sends one path north between the beams and another west along the base ledge.

Triples of marks correspond to hand tickets at hearings. Hand force only unseats instrument probes. The selected row drops a little on each transfer mark. There is no loose transfer hardware.

## `selector_aisle`

A selector rises out of the walkway like a long narrow sewing machine. A loop dial has three slots. A second lever moves between EAST EARTH and WEST STONE. Further north a cage holds the engagement controls.

The live writ gives the gate lock a matching notch and clears the selected branch for engagement. Other settings can be made, but cannot engage that work. The dials stay where left. A running transfer locks both. The side doors lead southeast to Daywell and west into a dim quiet cell.

## `daywell`

The hill closes over this little square except at the top, where a cut admits slow daylight. A length of the nearest signal line appears to levitate until its two thin bearings come into view.

The northwest gate uses the same interlock as the main bearing gate. The bar is transmitted around the north approach. It cannot be operated with a blank from the street side.

## `west_shore`

The water has worn a concave line along the stone groins. South of the line the store floors sit black and damp. North, a raised grated road leaves the shore for the hill. The old crossing is west, the receiving house east.

The low store floor is west relief discharge. Once discharged, gray mud surrounds open doors. The footways are above it. Iss knows how high up the Span dry storage starts and can kick off a move with four marks' notice.

## `west_head`

A row of wedges leads up to a receiving block backed by rock. Under the block is a scored gap with two grooves. A bleed lever is engraved HOLD on the near side and RIVER on the far.

The red shoe matches the west heel socket. It can be withdrawn while untested. Testing uses the hand wheel. **Plate:** "Vent to river. Test below second black. Set shoe home before load." Forcing the wheel at higher water with the vent at HOLD splits the heel and the shoe. At other closed-test settings the bend stops and clicks. The bleed stays open while seated or the seat unsets. It sends silt through the store footing when the new load displaces the contents of its face.

## `keel_walk`

The dim ledge is cut into the bow of the stone. Its gate west backs against high ground so a user needn't walk around the north approach.

The holes bored to take force readings are filled with fixed gauge rods. The west route stays open once the latch is withdrawn at Bearing Gate. The glass rods are indicators. They bear their own weight.

## `grate_road`

Rain falls through inches of grooved iron. Below the rails, dark water takes longer to arrive than the eye expects. The north ramp rises to the Observing Bridge.

A red stripe leads to the bearing side door. The west receiver is reached via the shore road to the south. The lane marked by black toward the deep shore is a shut drainage curb.

## `observing_bridge`

Small etched lines on the handrail match the rhythm of the basin's water marks. A bronze flag on the works is legible across the gorge. It has a pale stripe on one face and red on the other.

Before engagement the flag is turned narrow. An active circuit throws a red stripe. Complete gives a pale one. Failure leaves the flag below the rail. From here observers can see which relief channel has run. There's no view into the lower kitchens.

## `commit_cage`

A grid of wire keeps a hand out of a narrow part of the apparatus. A red engagement lever and a black hold lever operate opposite stops on the same shaft. A little slot is long enough for an embossing ribbon.

**Engagement plate:** "Keep the old dog in until the third tooth drops. Engage only at first black or below, with three marks of dry head. Black stop holds what the old dog still carries." A running transfer can be stopped here before the third tooth falls. Second black during engagement, unseating the receiver, or releasing the old dog drops the live shunt. The cage is on high ground. A good commissioning removes the old load automatically and prints a metal return band.

**Return band after engagement:** "19. Upper load passed to [EAST EARTH or WEST STONE]. Lower foot remains occupied. Lodge at the address." A blunt end with no perforations is the part a resident can read after the band is fixed in its slot.

## `quiet_cell`

A little upright arm stands in a body-length recess. In the absence of the bearing's motion the room would be silent. There is a dangerous bright edge on the arm where it has moved sideways through stone.

**Underside of arm:** "Manual release. Drop live load at once. Stand clear of sweep." This is the old holding dog. Releasing it early collapses the lower line and ruins transfer on 19. An operator at the lever takes a blow while the load is present. After the machine has completed the dog is already open.
