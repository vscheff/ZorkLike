# Lower households

## `under_ledge`

With light: A ladder track has been cut all the way down to natural rock. Its open end is just where the standing cage would stop. A wide scrape goes east from the stone. Fainter marks make a shallow diagonal northeast, and a squarer cut goes north.

An up/down call strip can be found beside a drop at the metal side. The return duct is north and gives safe refuge above the inlet's level. The flare below the outside opening is exposed.

## `return_duct`

With light: Stone shanks that look like beams were left projecting from the cut. There is more space between them than they appear to leave. The topmost has the other side of the iron inlet eye, and a climbing channel goes to it.

The passage is high above the outside shelf. Setting either lifting tool under the eye from inside also operates it. Up is travel out through the shelf, so high water blocks the exit that way. The stone transmits distinctive groups of knocks near the bell bed.

## `rung_yard`

With light: A small worn square is set out with iron climbing rungs. Each rung of a hanging stool has been polished by a separate pair of feet. Two rows of low handrails could have belonged to a children's game.

Residents use the footholds to carry heavy household things where a wall constricts the walk. The cloth and kitchen areas have lamps of their own. The east way toward Wire Run stays dark. A discarded label attached to the rail reads "six lived pads."

## `cloth_door`

A woven curtain closes off the draft but stops a few inches above the floor. On the other side you can hear the ends of spoons touching a table. East of the doorway is a rectangle of warm light. Low lamps sit in caged brackets.

The curtain can be pushed aside without taking it. Mera occasionally passes through on the way around the lower hearths.

## `low_kitchen`

A crack in the plaster lets steam out through the whole length of the wall. Small bead tassels stand out of its width where the pots have hung for years. Knives are on wooden slips with names burned into their handles.

Mera knows the delivery seal by its half-wheel. Show her the slip while she is physically present and civil to receive the household impression. She heard that her family moved out from someone who'd just eaten here. She can witness the foot's usual load and the missing address. If warned on the basis of the old link and the grille is open, she can direct households up the safe way.

## `address_wall`

The rows of house marks run up the wall like a crooked stair. At the top is a short horizontal socket with room for a band. A little shelf lamp catches its hammered lip.

Searching the family rack below the slot reveals a rocking tile. Stale, serviceable foil lies in a shallow groove behind it. A finger can lift it after finding the slack edge. **Socket plate:** "Where the load ended, lodge the works return." It's keyed for an authentic fired band. Unused foil doesn't fit. The record belongs here, where people can see that the old line has stopped honestly. After transfer, the knocks are quieter and have an ordinary domestic cadence.

## `occupied_sill`

Broad wooden doorstones overlap the base of the wall. Some have been pressed down by ordinary foot traffic. Springs under them respond at six separate points. The lintels stay readable in the household light.

The figures at each pad repeat the first six house marks on Address Wall. A house can have a whole family and only one pad. An impression can be made at the Settlement Shaft when all pads bear, including in their weighted warning configuration. At the outset the pads carry the houses and incoming bearing on separate planes. The frame goes east to its press. If the underlying line collapses the route across this room blocks. If the line is properly relieved, the sill straightens the width of a fingernail.

## `sixth_pier`

With light: The head of a fat stone column goes farther into the ceiling than it does into the floor. In between there's barely room to walk past it. Marks on a thin plate disappear upward under the great glaze.

The mounting has "19" cast in it and a small pair of pricks separated from a larger one. The force goes into the pier. Old inspection scars show how the load crosses it even while the houses use their own pads. The mounting cannot be levered with the runner's equipment.

## `wire_run`

With light: Five wires seem to run together until half of them disappear into a new patch in the wall. The channel in the floor stops against an old bell frame to the south.

The wires carry mechanical signals. They are hooked hard into the trunk edge ties, too short to serve as a grapnel. Tapping and listening can confirm which of two nearest lines reached up to the old notice.

## `bell_bed`

With light: Unclappered bells hang on thick flat straps, mouths almost to the floor. They ring quietly when the straps feel a pulse. The masonry pad at the outer end clears the ordinary rise.

The nearest little bell repeats two short knocks, space, and one longer note. Its leg continues toward the Sixth Pier. The loudest outer bell repeats ten times quickly to signal a collapse. The enclosed beds are unsafe once the old link fails. A detached bell cannot reproduce a physical trace.

## `settlement_shaft`

With light: Thin blades from different springs all run into the bed of one small press. Every blade stands a little proud of the face. An adjustable pad in front of them is the size of a strip of foil.

Marked "House load when all six bear". Put in the spare foil and pull to make an impression, returned to hand. Local house weight leaves the dimples. An existing strip remains genuine after the neighborhood changes, but the die has no full live pull after structural loss. Water trace leaves are the wrong kind of foil.

## `uphill_gap`

With light: Feet have taken a rising line around the face of the rock. The iron grille catches only on a thumb latch on this side. Old chalk bars distinguish the cool safe stone from the paths that sink under the bearing.

The runner can unhook the grille from here and go up. There is sure ground toward the kitchen or southwest toward the landing even after structural failure. The safe hand path follows the right wall. Warnings give the households six marks to reach high ground.
