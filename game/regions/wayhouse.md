# Wayhouse

## `vestibule`

**Opening at mark zero.** The packet boat has gone. Rain rattles in the tin tube by the door, and every so often the floor answers with a single knock. The clerk sent you here with a folded delivery slip and the assurance that Number Six would be easy to find.

A tin lamp stands on the sill. The weighing room is north, the stone walk south. A door to the dry store stands open to the west, and a ladder goes up into the loft.

On return, report the tube, sill and local items only as they stand. The tube is broken open at ankle level. It drains the front roof to the south and gives off a different note when water covers its outlet. The floor knock is a faint compression tick from the line far below. There is no visible means of calling back the packet boat.

**Delivery slip inner text:** "6. Return if occupied." There is half of a toothed circle at the bottom and a space large enough for an impression. A clerk wrote "WAYHOUSE FOOT" along the fold. There is no name. The other half of the seal remains on the dispatch book off map.

## `weigh_room`

The old travelers' scale has been stripped for parts. Its iron counterpan survives, sticking out of the wall at waist height. A hatch in the floor bucks slightly on every distant tick.

Six brass divisions are struck on the pan; the pointer climbs as weight is put into it. Just below the open catch is a tapered gap. Inspecting the hatch shows a cable running to the pan, a rising latch when loaded, and fixed grips to lift from the well side. Below the hatch there is a foothold at ladder length, so a physical opening is sufficient. An ordinary pull from above lifts the leaf only to the fastened latch. At rest with six divisions or more, the latch actually holds open. An ordinary wedge under a shut lid doesn't disengage it. With the latch actually up, the steel shim fits the taper and can prop it even when the pan is emptied. Either general hand placement or the ship scale test is safe.

## `dry_store`

The door has a horsehair brush where a threshold ought to be. Shelves mark out the heights of goods removed long ago with the pale parts of the wall.

In the open, an oil tin for the lamp, the six-division iron counterweight, and a tapered steel shim. The oil tin carries a repair stencil for the market stand. The iron can be moved without tools and put down on any firm surface. A stock chart nailed to the wall would tell an observant player that a stone displacer jug is four divisions when sealed.

## `sill_walk`

A long wall clips the wind off at shoulder height. Below it, paired staples are sunk into the basin slope. At low water the staples end in a little paved step farther south. At higher water, report the corresponding covering of the stones and the strength of the current.

An iron plate on the safe side reads "No foot past second black." A line of black heads marks the wash exit. Water mounts from the step toward the eastern drain, with the bank offering a place to be thrown clear at the wall.

## `sixth_step`

You can stand on the last of six shallow risers and put both palms against a window where a doorway used to be. A brass plate reads 6. A slip of official paper, sealed against wet with a thin skin, has been pushed between the stones.

**Notice:** "Service suspended above. For foot of line see lower return. No vacancy to be presumed from exposure." The former delivery hole has been packed with lead. Knocking gets the sound of a single thick wall. The steps to the Registry are open north, the first houses stand east.

## `old_ferry`

A narrow room with a curved counter and ticket slots too shallow for modern paper. The track of bare feet below the counter is deeper than any boot track outside.

The ferry box's lock is flat, with two offset teeth. Its hinges are cast into the counter. Its key was stored out of the spray in the Post Loft. On opening, there is a passenger list and a splintered fare counter. **List:** "Supplemental crossing, after vacancy. Six down. Nineteen, lower foot, six fares paid." Six names, including Aven, follow. It is genuine corroboration at the meeting board and an independently interpretable clue to the old branch. Show the counter to Nemi for a personal account of the day. The roll of sailcloth is beside the box in public reach.

## `post_loft`

The ladder arrives in a square of daylight behind a retired flag mast. Pigeon down has found its way into each of the tin slots except one.

Inspecting/searching the lapped boards shows an end with a finger gap, white scuffing, and a dull flat glint behind the unused slot. A hook or reeved chain pulls the ferry key clear. The same reach can lift the pintle in the lapped boards, exposing an east catwalk to the roofs. The gap gives direct line of sight but a hand cannot get around the turned edge. Once open the boards slide against a stop and stay.

## `hatch_well`

With light: A steep ladder hangs through the short ceiling. A railed platform looks down into a square shaft. A plate with a raised U and D is bolted to the floor beside a finger-sized calling chain. The work cut to the northeast was made with a much wider chisel.

The platform is above all tides. A boot can find the calling chain by touch. The cradle gate has a lip very unlike the rail; if no floor aligns, the lip projects above the rail. The mechanism only moves when the drive has a proper fork or an anchored chain replacing it. From here either calling position may be commanded, but boarding is down only with the car at its upper stop. The hatch back up can be drawn from here. At the foot of the service hatch the current latch may be slipped temporarily without altering its balance.

## `wash_step`

A flight of black headed staples stops on a single washed flagstone. Each retreat of the water leaves threads of green glassy weed on its eastern edge.

Low water only. No shelter on the step. On a damaging rise water forces a body north over the apron toward Sill Walk. Loose carried things may be swept off. The safer northern step is a single quick withdrawal.

## `culvert_mouth`

With light: A lintel made from a single ferry plank. Fingernail scrapes show where smaller things were dragged through to the Tail Drain.

The mouth floods. A long beam in the ceiling can be felt and gives the approximate bearing of north. Water at two knocks runs northward into the Tail Drain and throws anything caught here against its sill. The passage doesn't reach the lower rooms except by the old inlet farther north.
