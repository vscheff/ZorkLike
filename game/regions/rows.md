# Terraced rows

## `market_stair`

An outside stair divides a brick house between the first- and second-floor doors. The older tread is concave. Number Six is visible to the west whenever the laundry lifts. The middle landing joins the square east and the lane north. An upper flight goes to the roof walk.

The boards on the north rail carry a dinner bell time and the key to colors of the pulley flags, including a red stripe for a load being brought onto a bearing. There is no current red flag at the outset.

## `day_market`

Little roofs lean over a passage that has never been wide enough for two handcarts. You can smell the oil room before you see the doorway east. The kitchen to the north has stacked its empty crocks in the entrance.

No vendor has a critical object in reserve. A few will talk about service rents. Nemi spends part of each circuit here and at the kitchen.

## `oil_room`

Oil from a shallow tray runs back down a hand-cut groove in the counter. A fixed wick knife gives the oil a sharp hot smell. Clean linen lies over the near stand rail. No charge is levied on the local runner's lamp.

The stand's spigot refills a portable stoppered tin. It is always within physical reach. The stand is a cistern replenished by ordinary business beyond the simulated consumption of one lamp; it is not a source of portable bulk quantities. Wax kept for wicks is enough to dress the scrap of sailcloth into a packing alternative. Inspecting the rail shows the grade of clean linen.

Nemi recognizes the fare counter by the split where a round fare became a half fare. She ferried six workers home after the announced vacancy, and knows Mera paid. She gives a small prop coin regulars used to tally passage by sliding it across the counter only as a demonstration; the coin is fixed to its example string.

## `common_kitchen`

The benches are laid out for three shifts. Every cup stands upside down except one with its handle missing. A sealed stone jug sits under the third bench. It is heavy enough to need two hands when poured but can be carried at the hip.

The jug is a stone displacer, filled permanently with shot; it is no source of drinking water or loose shot. Four divisions incised under the handle. People here know that the upper roof was once priced by how much the houses below could hold, though they may not believe those houses are occupied.

## `loom_lane`

Wooden beams poke right through both the fronts and the backs of the houses. The weavers hang damp widths from those that still sound hollow when tapped. The Meeting Room stands north with its door pushed back into the limewash.

Tave sometimes checks an old rent column marked by nails on the beam ends. There is an unexplained sixth notch with no corresponding price. Meeting records here can give you a public route through a disputed history but they cannot fit the wax or drive the works.

## `low_balcony`

The far railing used to be the sill of a shop. Through it you look down onto rows of oil buckets. Immovable copper rings sewn into the stone were for hoisting furniture without using the narrow stair.

Examine a patched rent notch to find the same overstrike struck over a 6 here as at the Sixth Step. Looking downward from here is safe. Nothing is concealed in the buckets.

## `union_room`

The long table is made from two front doors. Hand-sized bills lie flat under nails on the meeting board. Room is left on the lower rail for disputes with the Registry.

The carbon order or the old ferry list can be pegged to the board, placing the original in public possession and marking the narrower fact it shows. The publish flag records a genuine suppressed historical record in public possession. With the carbon it is the rent order's actual figures. With the ferry list it is the route's passengers and date. Tave will tell the story either way. The public facts never serve as the pressure proof.

## `roof_walk`

Rain gutters cross an open court in a carpenter's crisscross of repaired bridges. There is a clearer walk north and a shop balcony east. From the back, the lapped boards at the old Wayhouse are indistinguishable from the roof sheathing until the loft panel is open.

From here the flat southern bowl and the earth on two sides of it are legible. A stitched yellow line on the east bank is bedding for the irrigation trough, not a footway across the basin. The Long Span is a much wider structure and points west.

## `hearing_porch`

The flags from the stairs hung here long enough to leave rusty stripes. The adjoining public hearing floor is east through a wide opening.

Small printed relief "Manual filing when the assessor is away" points east. Nothing prevents public entry. A clerk's announcement from inside can be heard from this threshold.

## `weight_stair`

Stone steps come in groups of four so carts can rest sideways on the landings. From this height the orchard seems to be on a raised plate. The way west descends to the square, and the Long Span starts east.

A footnote cut in the guard stone is a formal notice for the loading crews. Under state changes, newly set public flags can be seen from here, with no readout of what's happening below.

## `tally_square`

A low empty rectangle exists where rain needs a chance to run off the roofs. Menus and pay scales have been carved into the same post. The water crew has left room to pass south.

Its deeply scored bell rim marks twelve strokes in a complete basin cycle. The fourth stroke after the lowest flat gives the first wet footing. The short scale at Gauge Nook is an authoritative guide to current water.

## `span_foot`

Two wagon lengths of slope lead onto long pavement set like a comb. The roadway east takes two marks to cross. The basin side of the crossed deck is barred, but you can see exactly where the west bleed spits out below it.

This is safely above either discharge. If Iss's warning is complete, evidence of their work here is loading trays, covered crates, and a kettle standing outside on a wheeled board. If west has discharged without warning, wet gray packing strips are piled up at the foot.
